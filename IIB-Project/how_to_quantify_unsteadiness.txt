In high-speed aerodynamics and fluid mechanics, shockwave unsteadiness (the rapid, oscillating motion of a shock wave) is typically quantified using a mix of statistical, spectral, and spatial experimental or computational metrics.
Here are the primary ways to quantify this phenomenon, grouped by the type of data analysis:

Spatial & Displacement Metrics

These metrics focus on how far and where the shock wave moves over time.
• Shock-Position Standard Deviation (\(\sigma _{x_{s}}\)): Measures the root-mean-square (RMS) deviation of the shock position from its mean location. A higher standard deviation indicates a larger physical footprint of the oscillation.
• Intermittency Factor (γ): Quantifies the fraction of time a specific spatial point spends upstream versus downstream of the shock. It transitions smoothly from 0 (always upstream/unshocked) to 1 (always downstream/shocked) through the shock foot region.
• Shock-Foot Footprint (\(\Delta x_{foot}\)): The total spatial extent along a wall or boundary layer where the shock wave moves. It is often defined as the region where 0.05 < γ < 0.95.

Statistical Pressure Metrics

Because shock waves introduce a sharp jump in pressure, wall-mounted or flow-field pressure transducers are excellent tools for quantification.
• Wall Pressure RMS Peaks (\(\sigma _{p}\) or \(p_{rms}^{\prime }\)): The unsteadiness creates a distinct, massive peak in the RMS of wall pressure fluctuations right within the shock foot. The height and width of this peak directly quantify unsteadiness intensity.
• Skewness and Kurtosis: Tracking the higher-order statistical moments of pressure signals near the shock. A highly skewed probability density function (PDF) reveals intermittent loading caused by the shock passing back and forth over a sensor.

Frequency & Spectral Domain Metrics

These metrics identify the time scales and physical drivers (e.g., downstream separation bubbles vs. upstream turbulent boundary layers) of the motion.
• Power Spectral Density (PSD): Plots the energy of pressure or position fluctuations against frequency. Shock unsteadiness typically manifests as a large concentration of energy at low frequencies (Strouhal numbers \(Sr_L \sim 0.01 \text{ to } 0.05\)).
• Strouhal Number (St or Sr): A dimensionless frequency used to categorize the unsteadiness scales. It is calculated as St = fL/U, where f is the dominant oscillation frequency, L is a characteristic length (like separation length), and U is the freestream velocity.
• Coherence Function: Used to determine the causal relationship between upstream turbulent fluctuations and the shock motion, or between the shock and downstream separation bubble breathing.

Advanced Data-Driven Methods

Modern fluid dynamics heavily relies on extracting coherent structures from experimental images (like Schlieren or PIV) or CFD datasets.
• Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD): Isolates the spatial modes and temporal coefficients of the flow. The lowest-frequency POD modes usually capture the bulk energy of the shock wave's breathing or sweeping motion.
• Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD): Extracts modes based on pure frequencies. This allows researchers to isolate the exact spatial structure of the shock wave oscillating at a specific resonant frequency.
